How to use College Money Manager

You type in what you have, what's coming in, and what's going out. The app tells you whether your money lasts until the end of the semester, and how much you can spend each week so it does.

Will my money last?The top box gives you a yes, or the date you'd run out.
How much can I spend a week?Your weekly number: what's safe to spend on food, fun, and everything else.

It takes about 10 minutes the first time. You'll want your bank app open (for your balance and recent spending) and a rough idea of your rent and bills. Exact numbers help, but good guesses work fine to start.

00 · Start hereSee it work before you type anything

The first time you open the app, there's a Try a sample student button at the top. It loads a made-up student with a job, rent, a car, two weeks of spending, and a spring-break goal. Look around: change their rent, delete a dinner, and watch the numbers move. When you're ready, press Clear everything at the bottom of the page and put in your own.

The app has two tabs. My budget is your plan. Learn is six short lessons on budgeting, each with a question to check yourself.

Everything you type is saved in this browser on this device, and nowhere else. No account, no sign-in. If you switch phones or clear your browser data, it starts empty.

01–04 · Fill in four boxesTell it where you stand

On a laptop the boxes are on the left and your results are on the right. On a phone your results come first; scroll down to the boxes.

1 · Where you are now

  • Money you have today. What's in checking plus any cash you plan to live on. Leave out savings you don't want to touch.
  • Semester ends. Your last day on campus. It's set to Dec 11 for fall; change it if yours is different. Everything is figured from today to this date.

2 · Money coming in

Press + Add money coming in for each source: a paycheck, money from family, a refund that hasn't landed yet. Give each one an amount and how often it comes.

How oftenWhen the app counts it
every weekOne week from today, then every 7 days
every 2 weeksTwo weeks from today, then every 14 days
every monthThe 1st of each month
one timeToday (use it for money you're sure is coming, like a refund)

Paychecks: use what lands in your account after taxes, not your hourly rate times hours.

3 · Money going out

This box has two parts, because there are two kinds of spending.

Bills that repeat are the same every time: rent, phone, car insurance, subscriptions. Press + Add a bill, type the name, the amount, and how often. Monthly bills are counted on the 1st. When you finish typing a bill's name, Jev picks a category for it (see How Jev sorts). You can change it from the menu under the bill.

What you spent is everyday spending: food, gas, going out. Type the amount and what it was for in one line, then press Add (or Enter).

  • The date defaults to today. Change it to log something from earlier in the week.
  • Log at least a few days of spending. The app needs it to work out your pace, which is what the pie chart and the red runway line come from.
  • Wrong category? Pick the right one from the menu. Your choice always wins, and the chip changes to you chose.
  • Made a mistake? The × on the right removes the line.
Don't log rent or other bills here too. If they're in "Bills that repeat," logging them again counts them twice.

4 · Savings goals

A goal is money you want to still have at the end of the semester: spring break, a cushion for a surprise bill, next semester's books. Give it an amount and how much you've already set aside. The app takes what's left to save off the top before it works out your weekly number. That's called paying yourself first (Lesson 4 on the Learn tab).

05 · Read your resultsWhat the app tells you

The top box: will it last?

The colored bar on top tells you at a glance. Green means you make it to the end of the semester. Red means you don't at your current pace, and the box names the day you'd run out. Under it are three numbers:

NumberWhat it means
Weekly numberWhat you can spend each week on everything that isn't a bill or a goal, and still make it. This is the one to remember.
Your paceWhat you've actually been spending per week, from what you logged. Compare it with the weekly number: if your pace is higher, you'll run short.
Time leftDays and weeks from today to the end of the semester.

Press Show the math to see exactly how the weekly number was worked out. Here's the sample student's, on October 27 (the numbers shift as the semester goes on):

Your pace is everything you logged divided by the days you've been logging, times 7. If you've logged fewer than 7 days, it divides by 7 days anyway, so one big day doesn't make your pace look huge.

Where your money goes: the pie chart

Each slice is a category. Hover over a slice (or look at the list beside it) for the dollar amount and the share. Two views:

  • What I logged: just your everyday spending, exactly as you entered it.
  • A month, bills in: a typical month, with your bills plus a month of everyday spending at your pace. This is usually the eye-opener, because rent tends to be the biggest slice by far.

Under the pie, Needs and wants splits what you logged into the two. Show as a table gives the same numbers as a list, if that's easier to read.

Your runway

The runway chart shows your balance on every day from today to the end of the semester. It steps up when money comes in, drops when a bill is due, and slides down a little every day from everyday spending. Hover or drag across it to see the balance on any day.

At your logged paceSpending exactly your weekly number

If the solid line crosses $0 before the dashed "Semester ends" line, a dot marks the day you'd run out. The dashed green line shows what happens if you spend exactly your weekly number: it ends the semester above zero, with your goals still saved. The gap between the two lines is the change you'd need to make.

What stands out

A short list of things the app noticed: you're over your weekly number by a certain amount, eating out is more than groceries, rent is more than half your month, and so on. Each note says Why underneath, so you can see which numbers it came from. These are written by simple rules in the app, not by an AI.

Print my plan

At the bottom of the page. It prints your results and the tables (not the input boxes), which is handy to stick on the fridge or bring to a meeting with a financial aid advisor.

06 · How Jev sortsThe AI part, and what it doesn't do

Jev is an AI model from TypeSafe AI. It's different from a chatbot: it never writes sentences. The app gives it a short question and a list of options, and it sends back a percentage for each option. In this app it answers two questions about everything you log:

  1. Which category does this belong in? (rent & housing, groceries, eating out, and so on)
  2. Is this a need or a want for a college student?

The chip next to each entry shows how sure Jev was:

Jev 100%Jev 41%guessyou chose
  • Jev 100%: Jev was sure. Click it to see the percentage for every category.
  • Jev 41% in orange: Jev was split between categories. Worth a look.
  • guess in orange: Jev couldn't be reached (no internet, or the service was down), so the app matched words against a list instead.
  • you chose: you picked the category yourself. Jev won't change it.
What gets sent: only the words you typed for an entry, like "chipotle with roommates." Never the amount, the date, your balance, or anything else. Jev's answer for the same words is remembered, so "starbucks" is only asked once. At the bottom of the spending list, What Jev is asked shows the exact questions.

Need or want is a judgment call. Jev's answer is a starting point for you to agree or disagree with, not a grade. A phone plan is a need; the newest phone probably isn't. You decide.

07 · The Learn tabSix short lessons

Each lesson takes two or three minutes:

  1. Read the short intro at the top.
  2. Watch the picture build. Press Play and it moves through the steps on its own, or use the ← → buttons to go at your own speed. The words for each step are always shown underneath, and Transcript shows them all at once.
  3. Check yourself with one question at the bottom: work out a number, sort some items, or pick an answer. You get feedback right away and can try again as many times as you like.

08 · Make it a habitA first session, then five minutes a week

First session, about 20 minutes

  1. 3 min
    Load the sample student. Change their rent to $500 and watch the top box and the runway change.
  2. 2 min
    Clear everything. Enter your balance and your semester's end date.
  3. 5 min
    Add what's coming in and your bills. Check the categories Jev picked for your bills.
  4. 5 min
    Open your bank app and log the last week of everyday spending.
  5. 3 min
    Add one savings goal, even a small one. Read your weekly number and "What stands out."
  6. 2 min
    Do Lesson 1 on the Learn tab and check that your weekly number makes sense to you.

Every week after that

  • Update Money you have today from your bank app.
  • Log the week's spending, or at least the bigger things.
  • Look at one number: is your pace under your weekly number? If not, "What stands out" usually points to the easiest place to cut.

QuestionsGood to know

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a learning tool, and its numbers are estimates from what you type. For real decisions about loans, aid, or debt, talk to your school's financial aid office.

Where is my information stored?

Only in this browser, on this device. The only thing that leaves your device is the words of each spending entry, sent to Jev for sorting. Clearing your browser's site data or pressing "Clear everything" erases it.

Why does my weekly number change every day?

Because there are fewer days left. If you spend less than your weekly number this week, next week's number goes up a little, and the other way around. Update your balance and it catches up with real life.

My rent is due on the 5th, not the 1st.

The app counts every monthly bill and monthly income on the 1st, to keep things simple. Over a whole semester it comes out the same. It can make the run-out date a few days off if a big bill lands near the end.

What if my income changes week to week?

Use a lower, realistic amount for "every week." Planning on the low side means a good week becomes extra, instead of a slow week becoming a problem.

The runway shows only the dashed line.

The solid red line needs logged spending. Add a few days under "What you spent" and it appears.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. On a phone your results show first; scroll down for the boxes. Add the page to your home screen to open it like an app.
